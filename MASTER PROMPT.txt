MASTER PROMPT: BUILD WeNaijaHealth

1. ROLE AND MISSION

Act as a senior full-stack engineer, health-tech product architect, UI/UX designer, database engineer, and Nigerian health insurance systems specialist.

Build a functional, mobile-first web application called WeNaijaHealth.

Official name: WeNaijaHealth
Domain: wenaijahealth.com.ng
Campaign: Protect What Matters
Tagline: Your Work Shouldn't Cost You Your Health.

WeNaijaHealth is a digital health insurance access and enrolment-support platform designed for Nigeria's informal workforce, including market women, traders, artisans, petty traders, self-employed individuals, and other social and informal workers.

The platform should make health insurance easier to understand, easier to register for, and more accessible to people who face financial, literacy, language, and digital barriers.

Our model combines:

1. Simple, multilingual health insurance education.
2. Digital registration and eligibility guidance.
3. Connection to the National Health Insurance Authority (NHIA) and state health insurance schemes.
4. Financial assistance through a flexible reimbursement model, subject to programme rules and available funding.
5. Community-based registration through approved POS operators and registration assistants.
6. SMS and email notifications after registration.
7. Enrolment tracking and follow-up.
8. An administrative dashboard for managing participants, scheme referrals, reimbursements, and impact.

Build a real application, not merely a landing page or a collection of static mockups.

Where official agency integration, payment processing, or SMS/email credentials are unavailable, implement the necessary architecture and clearly indicate what requires configuration or authorisation.

2. UNDERSTAND OUR USERS

Our primary users are people who may not be comfortable using complicated websites.

Examples include:

- A market woman who sells tomatoes.
- A trader who owns a small shop.
- A tailor or mechanic.
- A petty trader who earns money daily.
- A self-employed worker with irregular income.
- An informal worker who cannot afford a large upfront insurance contribution.
- A person who speaks Hausa, Yoruba, or Igbo more comfortably than English.

Do not design the platform as though every user has a university education, a bank card, a smartphone with fast internet, or a fixed monthly salary.

The experience must be understandable to someone using a basic Android phone with limited digital literacy.

Product principle: If a market woman cannot understand how to use the platform without technical assistance, simplify the interface.

6. SIMPLE REGISTRATION FOR INFORMAL WORKERS

This is one of the most important features.

Build a guided registration experience using short screens, simple words, and large buttons.

Avoid presenting users with a long form containing dozens of fields.

Use a progress indicator such as:

Step 1 of 5 — About You

Step 1: Basic information

Collect only necessary information, potentially including:

- Full name.
- Preferred language.
- Phone number.
- Email address, if available.
- State.
- Local government area.
- Community or market.
- Preferred contact method.

Email must not be mandatory if a participant does not have an email address.

Do not reject a user simply because they lack an email address.

Step 2: Work information

Ask:

"What kind of work do you do?"

Provide large, selectable options:

- Market trader.
- Shop owner.
- Petty trader.
- Artisan.
- Farmer.
- Food seller.
- Transport worker.
- Self-employed.
- Other informal work.

Allow the user to select "Other" and describe their work in simple terms.

Do not require formal employment records, a salary slip, a business registration certificate, or proof of a formal employer unless a specific programme legitimately requires it.

Step 3: Current health insurance situation

Ask:

"Do you currently have health insurance?"

Options:

- Yes.
- No.
- I am not sure.

If yes, ask which scheme they use, where known.

If no or unsure, continue to scheme discovery.

Step 4: Household and coverage needs

Ask only questions relevant to the available insurance schemes.

For example:

- Are you looking for coverage for yourself?
- Are you interested in coverage for eligible family members?

Collect age, household information, identity details, or other eligibility data only when required by a specific scheme.

Do not invent universal eligibility requirements.

Step 5: Consent and review

Explain in the user's selected language:

- Why the information is being collected.
- Which organisation may receive it.
- How it will be used.
- Whether financial assistance is being requested.
- How to request help.
- How to access the privacy policy.

Provide separate consent options where appropriate for:

1. Creating a WeNaijaHealth account or registration record.
2. Sharing relevant information with an identified insurance scheme.
3. Requesting reimbursement or financial assistance.
4. Receiving SMS and email communications.

Do not use one vague consent checkbox to authorise every purpose.

Let the user review their information before submission.

After submission, generate a unique WeNaijaHealth reference number.

Example:

Your reference number: WNH-2026-000123

Use a unique, securely generated reference in the real implementation rather than hard-coding this example.

Display:

"Thank you for registering with WeNaijaHealth. We will help you understand the next steps. Your insurance enrolment and coverage must still be confirmed by the relevant authorised scheme."

7. FLEXIBLE REIMBURSEMENT AND FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE

Build a dedicated module for WeNaijaHealth's proposed flexible reimbursement model.

Our intended approach

We want to help eligible informal workers overcome the financial barrier to accessing health insurance.

Depending on the participant's eligibility, the available funding, and the agreement with the relevant scheme, WeNaijaHealth may support payment of an approved insurance contribution and allow the participant to reimburse WeNaijaHealth flexibly.

The purpose is to reduce the need for a participant to provide the entire contribution upfront.

Important: This is a proposed financial assistance model. Do not represent it as universally available, and do not promise that coverage begins before the scheme confirms activation.

Participant experience

After a user completes the initial registration, show:

"Would you like to explore financial assistance with your health insurance contribution?"

Options:

- Yes, I need assistance.
- I can pay the contribution myself.
- I would like to understand my options first.

If they request assistance, ask a few simple questions.

Examples:

- What amount could you comfortably repay?
- How often would you prefer to make payments?
- Would weekly or monthly payments be easier?
- Would you like to discuss a suitable arrangement with our team?

Do not automatically assume that every market worker receives a fixed income.

Allow flexible schedules that reflect irregular earnings, subject to the programme's approved terms.

Do not automatically approve an amount or payment schedule without the required eligibility and affordability review.

Reimbursement options

Support configurable arrangements such as:

- Weekly payments.
- Monthly payments.
- Other approved flexible schedules.
- Sponsored contributions, where a sponsor has committed funding.
- Subsidised enrolment, where a programme provides an approved subsidy.

These are potential arrangements, not guaranteed options for every participant.

Reimbursement dashboard for participants

Show:

- Approved assistance amount.
- Insurance contribution amount, where verified.
- Amount paid on the participant's behalf.
- Amount reimbursed.
- Outstanding balance.
- Agreed repayment schedule.
- Next payment date.
- Payment history.
- Current assistance status.
- Contact information for support.

Use simple explanations instead of complex financial terminology.

For example:

"You have reimbursed ₦2,000 of your approved ₦10,000 assistance amount. Your remaining balance is ₦8,000."

Only show such figures when supported by actual transaction records.

Financial status values

Use statuses such as:

- Assistance requested.
- Under review.
- Awaiting approval.
- Approved.
- Payment to scheme pending.
- Payment confirmed.
- Repayment schedule agreed.
- Repayment in progress.
- Fully reimbursed.
- Declined.
- Cancelled.

Do not confuse the participant's reimbursement status with their insurance coverage status.

Financial controls

The system must:

- Keep a complete transaction ledger.
- Record payment references.
- Prevent duplicate transactions.
- Require authorisation for financial approvals.
- Record who approved each transaction.
- Reconcile payments against evidence.
- Support refunds and reversals.
- Protect against unauthorised changes.
- Keep an audit trail.

Do not process real payments until a suitable payment provider and approved financial workflow have been configured.

Do not present WeNaijaHealth as a licensed lender, insurer, or financial institution unless it has the necessary authorisations.

Before launching this model, obtain legal and regulatory review and establish agreements covering the funds, repayments, defaults, cancellations, and responsibility for coverage.

9. AGENCY CONNECTION AND SECURE DATA TRANSFER

WeNaijaHealth intends to establish working relationships with the NHIA, Kwara State health insurance authorities, and other state schemes.

Design the architecture to support authorised data transfer.

Preferred integration hierarchy

1. Official API integration where the agency provides an API and authorises its use.
2. An approved secure referral or partner portal.
3. An authorised manual registration process where digital integration is unavailable.

Do not scrape private systems, bypass agency security, or automatically submit personal data to an unapproved endpoint.

Integration requirements

For every participating agency, define:

- Approved data fields.
- Consent requirements.
- Authentication method.
- Secure transmission protocol.
- Request and response format.
- Error handling.
- Duplicate detection.
- Enrolment status mapping.
- Reference-number mapping.
- Audit logging.
- Data retention rules.

Build an agency integration adapter so that different state schemes can use different interfaces without requiring the entire platform to be rewritten.

Enrolment status

Display separate statuses for:

- WeNaijaHealth registration completed.
- Information reviewed.
- Eligibility assessment pending.
- Submitted to the agency.
- Agency processing.
- Agency approval confirmed.
- Insurance coverage active.
- Registration declined or incomplete.

Only the relevant agency or an authorised source can confirm official enrolment and coverage.

Do not show a participant as insured just because their information has been entered into WeNaijaHealth.

14. SECURITY AND DATA PROTECTION

The platform will process personal information in Nigeria.

Implement privacy by design and consider the requirements of the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and other applicable rules.

Required controls:

- Data minimisation.
- Explicit, understandable consent.
- Role-based access control.
- Secure authentication.
- Server-side authorisation.
- Password hashing through a reputable authentication system.
- Input validation.
- Rate limiting.
- Secure session handling.
- Protection against common web vulnerabilities.
- Encryption in transit.
- Appropriate encryption at rest.
- Secure secret management.
- Audit logging.
- Data retention and deletion procedures.
- Restricted access to financial information.
- Protected backups.
- Secure data export.
- Appropriate incident-response procedures.

Do not collect sensitive identification documents unless necessary for a legitimate registration process and appropriately protected.

Never collect or store insurance portal passwords, OTPs, banking PINs, or unnecessary medical records.

Do not send personal or sensitive information in SMS or email messages beyond what is necessary.

Do not claim legal compliance has been certified without a proper assessment.
